Rajasthan 2026

Figures as of 21 February 2026, when the final roll was published.

Overview

    This page is in English only for now. Every figure on it is the Election Commission's or the Chief Electoral Officer's own.

    Find a constituency or district

    An assembly constituency is the area that elects one member of the state assembly. Rajasthan has 200 of them. Type a constituency's name or number, or a district, and its figures appear below with a link you can send.

    Each constituency is shaded by the change in its roll of general electors, which is the roll without service voters, from the 2023 election to the final roll of March 2026. The change is counted for every 100 electors on the 2023 roll. The darkest fell most, and the lightest fell least or grew. Tap a constituency for its figures. Boundaries drawn from DataMeet's assembly constituency map (CC BY 2.5 India), simplified.

    The roll, step by step

    The roll before the revision is the list of electors when the revision began. The draft roll is the list published after the enumeration forms came back, and a name whose form did not come back was left off it. Names were then added and deleted on claims and objections, and the result is the final roll. These are the figures the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO), who keeps the state's rolls for the Election Commission, published for the 199 constituencies revised together. Anta was revised later, after its bye-election, and is outside them. The bars start above zero so that the changes can be seen.

    Rajasthan's dates, as they were set and as they were moved, are drawn beside every other state's on How the revision was run.

    The reasons

    Each name left off the draft carries one of three reasons. The reasons were published for the state only, in lakh (one lakh is 100,000) and rounded. A reason is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it.

    Per 100 electors, by district

    No count of the names left off has been published below the state. The rolls themselves are published by constituency, so this chart compares two of them. Each dot is one constituency's change in its roll of general electors, from the roll the 2023 election was held on to the final roll of 12 March 2026, grouped by district. Counting per 100 electors on the 2023 roll puts large and small constituencies on one scale. A fall of 5 per 100 means the roll is a twentieth smaller. The change takes in more than two years of new voters, deaths and moves as well as the revision, so it is a different count from the names left off. Below zero, the roll grew. Hover or tap a dot for its name.

    Women

    Each dot is a constituency. Its place across is women's share of its 2023 roll, and its height is women's share of its final roll in 2026. A dot below the dashed line is a constituency where women are now a smaller share of the roll than they were. Hover or tap a dot for its name.

    The same by district. Each line joins a district's share of women on the 2023 roll to its share on the final roll. The districts where it fell most are named and drawn in red.

    Against the 2023 margins

    The winning margin is the winner's votes less the runner-up's. Each dot is a constituency. Its place across is the margin its 2023 winner won by, and its height is the fall in its roll from 2023 to the final roll. Above the dashed line, the roll fell by more than the margin. That means the change was large enough to decide the seat, and it says nothing about whether it would have. Below zero, the roll grew.
