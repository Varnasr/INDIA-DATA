Looking closer at a state's figures

This page is in English only.

This page works through one state, Tamil Nadu, in detail. It sets out what the state's pages and downloads hold, how each figure is worked out, which other datasets can be set beside the electoral roll, how the data are checked and what they cannot show. It then describes how the public hearings held on the revision were run, and how an audit of a few polling booths can be started. For how the pages are laid out and how to find a place, see How to use this site.

What a state's pages and downloads hold

Tamil Nadu has figures for all 234 Assembly constituencies and 38 districts. They come from the Chief Electoral Officer's own constituency tables and the Election Commission's result pages, with the 2021 results from the TCPD dataset and the counts after the draft from The Hindu. There are no figures for polling stations, and none for the names left off by age or by sex.

The workbook carries the constituency and district tables, the names left off by seat and reason, and the draft, final, election and January 2025 rolls by seat and sex. The Tamil Nadu figures

97,37,831 names were left off Tamil Nadu's draft roll of 19 December 2025, 15.2 for every 100 electors on the roll before the revision. The final roll of 23 February 2026 is 74,07,207 smaller than that roll, a fall of 11.6 for every 100. The Independent Young Citizens Collective put Tamil Nadu's loss at 74.07 lakh names at a press conference in Chennai on 10 November 2026 (The Hindu). That figure is this fall, which is a different count from the names left off.

The figures after the draft are state totals from The Hindu of 23 February 2026. The draft plus those additions less those deletions equals the final roll exactly.

The highest rate of names left off is in Anna Nagar, 42.2 per 100, and the lowest in Jayankondam, 4.3. Chennai holds 6.2% of the state's roll and 14.6% of the names left off, at 35.6 per 100. In 211 of the 234 seats the names left off are more than the 2026 winning margin. Counting only the names not marked dead, and taking each seat's own turnout, the number is 168. Those are the seats where the names left off were enough to change the result if they had all voted for the runner-up. The figures cannot show how any of them would have voted.

How a figure is worked out

Every figure starts from a table the Chief Electoral Officer or the Election Commission published, and every step after that is arithmetic anyone can redo from the downloads.

  1. Collect the source tables. For Tamil Nadu these are the names left off by constituency and reason, the draft, final, election and January 2025 rolls by constituency and sex, and the 2026 and 2021 results. Each source, its address and the date it was retrieved are recorded beside the data.
  2. Check each table against its own totals. The constituency rows must add to each district total, and the districts to the state total the Chief Electoral Officer printed.
  3. Join the tables on the constituency number, 1 to 234. Names are never used to join, because spellings differ between tables, and a table with a constituency number missing or repeated is not used until the gap is explained.
  4. Make the roll before the revision by adding the names left off to the draft. Tamil Nadu did not publish a constituency table of that roll.
  5. Compute rates for every 100 electors on the roll before the revision, for the names left off and for the fall from that roll to the final roll. Rates are kept to four decimals and shown to one.
  6. Add constituencies up to districts. A seat is compared only with its own district and the state, because each state counted differently.
  7. Look for patterns with rank correlation, and treat a relationship as clear of chance only when it passes the usual test for the number of places. A correlation shows that two figures move together. It does not show that one causes the other.

Three words are kept apart throughout, as About the data defines them. A name left off the draft is one whose enumeration form did not come back. A name deleted is one an officer took off the roll after the draft, through Form 7 or the officer's own notice. A fall in the roll is the difference between two published rolls, which counts neither of the other two on its own.

The figures are counts. No name, EPIC number or address of an elector appears in any table or download, and none links to a list of named electors.

Across Tamil Nadu's districts, the rate of names left off rises with district income (rank correlation 0.71, 38 districts), the share of households using clean cooking fuel (0.63), the urban share (0.62) and density (0.50), and falls as the sex ratio rises (minus 0.57). The last four are over the 32 districts NFHS-5 and the 2011 Census drew. Each clears the test for chance. Together they say more names were left off in the more urban and better-off districts. They do not say why any one name was left off.

Two cautions apply to any correlation. A relationship across districts says nothing about which people within a district were left off. And a roll compared with projected adults mixes two errors. A roll above the adult population holds names of people who have died, moved or been entered twice, and a roll below it may be missing adults who never enrolled as well as names wrongly left off.

Form 20 and polling station counts of the names left off are the two datasets most worth getting. Together they would take the analysis down to the polling station, which is the level at which a check at the door can be made.

How the data are checked

No unexplained difference in a count is accepted. A figure either adds up to its own source's total, or the difference is written down beside the data and the figure is marked for what it is. Where a source is too poor to use, the figure is left out and the page says so.

Other checks hold that each state's tables add to the totals in its raw sources, that each table finds every constituency number once, and that no published file holds an individual record.

Missing data

  1. A missing figure shows as a dash. Nothing is filled in by guess or interpolation.
  2. Every figure carries its kind, which is exact, rounded, net only, or from the press as against the Chief Electoral Officer's own table. A press figure in lakh is marked rounded.
  3. Each state's page says what its tables cannot show. For Tamil Nadu that is any split of the names left off by sex or age, and any figure below the constituency.
  4. Where a state published only a state or district total, no seat is given a figure of its own. For the comparison with winning margins the total is spread over the seats by electors, which gives how many seats could be over the margin and cannot say which seats they are.

When a source is wrong

Sources contradict themselves often, and each case is written down beside the figure. Some examples follow.

  • Karnataka's reported final roll was 44,322 more than its men, women and third gender electors added up to, close to the 44,176 service electors. The figure used is the sum, which counts the same electors as the draft, and the page says why.
  • A Dainik Jagran report on Uttarakhand's summary revision draft gave the rise in men and in women the wrong way round. The figures were taken from the two rolls, and the swap is recorded.
  • Rajasthan's revision covered 199 of its 200 seats, because Anta's was put off for its bye-election and held later under a separate order. The Chief Electoral Officer's figures come for 199 seats and for 200, and the two sets are kept apart.
  • Tamil Nadu's 2021 results come from the TCPD dataset. Set against Wikipedia, the margins differed in ten seats, and in five of them Wikipedia's margin did not equal its own winner's votes less the runner-up's, so the TCPD figures were kept.
  • Where the Chief Electoral Officer and a party give different counts, both are kept and the party's is labelled as the party's claim. In Tamil Nadu a DMK application to the Supreme Court put the logical discrepancy notices at 1.7 crore (The Hindu, 28 January 2026), and the Chief Electoral Officer put them at about 1.58 crore (The New Indian Express, 24 February 2026).

Corrections

When a published figure turns out to be wrong, the page is corrected, and Corrections says what it said, what it says now and why.

What the data cannot show

The Chief Electoral Officer's tables give the reason an officer wrote down for each name left off. Nobody has checked whether the elector was in fact dead, had moved or was enrolled twice, and the tables cannot settle it, because they hold totals and no names. That check can only be made at the door, which is what a public audit does.

The tables cannot show four more things.

  • They cannot show how many of the names left off were put back, since the names added after the draft include new voters and no table says how many of them had been left off.
  • They cannot show whether women, the old or the young were left off more often, since no split of the names left off by sex or age was published.
  • They cannot show how anyone left off would have voted, or why their name was left off.
  • They cannot show anything below the constituency until Form 20 and polling station counts of the names left off are obtained.

How the Jan Sunwais on the revision were run

The Events page lists Jan Sunwais held on the revision. The steps below are taken from the accounts that the organisers and the reporters who attended published, and each names its source. The fullest accounts are of the Delhi public audits by Jan Sarokar, the Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) and Voter Adhikar Manch in Mangolpuri, Okhla and Trilokpuri, and of the Telangana Voter Rights Forum's audit of two polling stations in Secunderabad Cantonment, Hyderabad.

  1. Pick a few polling booths and take their lists. The Mangolpuri audit took eight booths and used the 2025 Delhi Assembly roll as its baseline (The Wire). The Okhla audit worked from the lists of electors marked absent, shifted, dead or duplicate, the notice list and the deletion lists from before the revision (The Indian Express).
  2. Go door to door with volunteers. Mangolpuri covered nearly 10,000 voters over more than three weeks (The Wire). Okhla covered 1,579 voters in 307 households over a week (The Indian Express, while The Hindu gives 258 households). Trilokpuri covered 1,236 voters in 700 households over four days with about 40 volunteers (The Indian Express, eNewsroom).
  3. Record each person against the list as rightly listed, wrongly listed or not traced, with whether a form had been generated or collected and whether a notice had reached them. In Okhla, of 1,009 checked, the organisers counted 100 as wrongly marked, 434 as rightly listed and 475 as not traced (The Indian Express). In Trilokpuri the organisers counted 416 of the 1,236 verified as wrongly listed (The Indian Express), and eNewsroom notes that the Commission has not verified these findings.
  4. Report each finding as a count out of those checked, as the Okhla and Trilokpuri reports do, so that a reader can see the base.
  5. Hold the hearing. Residents registered at a desk and were given tokens, and their cases were heard by a panel of lawyers and activists. In Okhla more than 150 registered (The Hindu) and 153 had tokens by 5.45 pm (The Indian Express). The Booth Level Officers, the Electoral Registration Officer, the District Magistrate and the Commission were invited through the Chief Electoral Officer, and in Okhla they did not come (The Hindu).
  6. Turn findings into claims. A desk helped people fill Form 6 with a declaration that they were not first-time voters, and sent them to polling stations where Booth Level Officers were present, because the organisers could not collect the forms themselves (The Hindu, The Indian Express). The Hyderabad forum ran a help desk filing Form 6 "under protest" (The Hindu).

MKSS describes the method in general terms. The records are first obtained through RTI, then read aloud before the community and verified in the open. Officials answer in public, and a written note of the proceedings records each discrepancy with a deadline. MKSS warns of incomplete records, short notice and intimidation (MKSS).

Two cautions apply to any hearing. Reading out or publishing the names of electors left off exposes the people named. The framework for a public audit set out in an opinion piece in The Wire has the Booth Level Officer read out the names of deleted electors at the hearing, and the Congress has asked its units to read out deleted names at village chaupals by 15 December 2026 (Deccan Herald, 14 November 2026). Counts carry the findings of a hearing without exposing anyone. The Hyderabad forum loaded the lists into an app shared with volunteers (The Hindu), so anyone copying that should decide who holds the lists and when they are destroyed.

For the special summary revision that began in November 2026, the Commission's order of 15 October 2026 gives recognised parties each polling station's draft roll with the lists of additions, deletions and changes, and a printed list of claims and objections every week. The order names recognised parties as the ones to receive them. In Tamil Nadu claims and objections are open from 12 November to 11 December 2026, and until then those lists are the most current baseline for an audit there.

Starting an audit of your own

  1. Download tamil-nadu.xlsx, or the workbook for another state, from Downloads, and pick the seats to look at from the constituency table. In Tamil Nadu, Anna Nagar (42.2 per 100) and the other Chennai seats have the highest rates, and a seat near the state's 15.2 makes a fair comparison.
  2. Within a chosen seat, ask the Electoral Registration Officer for the polling station lists that the Commission's order of 15 October requires parties to be given, or obtain them through a recognised party. File RTI applications for the polling station counts of names left off by reason, and for Form 20.
  3. Run a small audit in two to four booths, following the six steps above, and keep the count of those checked, found rightly listed, wrongly listed and not traced.
  4. Help those wrongly left off to file Form 6 before claims close on 11 December 2026. News On AIR reported on 12 November 2026 that the Chief Electoral Officer had set special camps in Tamil Nadu on 21, 22 and 29 November 2026. Tamil Nadu's What to do now page sets out each step with the order it rests on.
  5. Publish the counts with how they were gathered, as totals with no names, so that a reader can see the base of every finding.
