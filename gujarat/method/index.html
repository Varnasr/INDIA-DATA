Gujarat 2026

Method

    This page is in English only for now. Every figure on it comes from the Election Commission or the Chief Electoral Officer, and where it reached us through the press, the page says so.

    What is counted

    The tables of the draft

    The Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) keeps Gujarat's electoral rolls for the Election Commission. The CEO's office printed two tables of the draft roll of 19 December 2025, one by district and one by assembly constituency (AC), the area that elects one member of the state assembly. Each gives the roll before the revision, the names left off under five reasons and the draft roll. These are the CEO's tables as published in the press. They are known from images of the printouts carried by DeshGujarat on 20 December 2025, which appear to have been posted by the CEO's office on X. The CEO's own website carries nothing on the revision. The article's text gives the same state figures as the tables.

    The roll before the revision

    The tables give each constituency's electors as of 1 October 2025, the roll the revision started from, names for the state. Every "per 100" on these pages is out of it.

    The five reasons

    The tables enter each name left off under one of five heads. They are death, untraceable or absent, permanently shifted, already enrolled (at another place) and other. The All India pages fold them into three groups for every state, with untraceable or absent, shifted and other counted together. A reason is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it.

    How the tables were checked

    The tables were read by eye from clean typeset images, and every figure is held by at least one sum. In every constituency the five reasons must add up to the names left off, and the roll before less the names left off must equal the draft. These are two separate checks, so a misread digit in any of a row's eight figures fails one of them. Each district's constituencies must add up to the district's printed total, in every column, and the districts to the printed state total of left off and on the draft. The district table was read separately and agrees with the constituency table district by district. The site is not built unless every check holds, and they all do.

    The final roll

    The final roll of 17 February 2026 is known from one table, by district, also printed by the CEO's office and published by DeshGujarat on 17 February 2026. For each district it gives the draft, the names added and deleted after it, the net change and the final roll. It has 34 districts, because it prints Vav-Tharad, a district formed from part of Banaskantha, on its own line. On these pages Vav-Tharad is added back into Banaskantha, so that the final table lines up with the 33 districts of the draft. The two together have exactly Banaskantha's draft. Vav-Tharad's draft equals that of three constituencies, Vav, Tharad and Deodar, so those are inferred to be its seats, but no table says so and these pages do not rely on it. There is no final roll by constituency.

    Electors the final table does not account for

    In every district the net change equals the names added less the names deleted, and every column adds up to the printed state total. But in districts the printed final roll is larger than the draft plus the net change, by electors in all. They are . The state total carries the same . Every one of those rows was read again from an enlarged image, and the digits are as printed. The table does not say why. These pages keep both figures as printed. The final roll is the printed final, and the change after the draft is the printed additions less deletions.

    The 2022 result

    The general election to the Gujarat assembly was held on 1 and 5 December 2022. The Election Commission's own results pages for it no longer answer, and its statistical report could not be reached. The winner, runner-up and votes of every seat are therefore from a published compilation of the Election Commission's results, checked seat by seat against a second compilation. The two agree on the margin and on the winner's and runner-up's parties in all 182 seats, and the seats by party equal the declared result (). The compilation's electors and total votes do not add up to the Election Commission's state figures, so they are not used, and no turnout is computed. The margin is the winner's votes less the runner-up's. NOTA, the option to vote for none of the candidates, is never counted as a runner-up.

    The map

    The map is drawn by district from DataMeet's assembly constituency map (CC BY 2.5 India), simplified, with each constituency placed in its district. A map by constituency was not possible. DataMeet's map has no shapes of their own for 20 city seats, Naranpura to Asarwa in Ahmedabad (45 to 56) and Surat North to Surat West (160 to 167). It draws each city as one block with no number, so a map by constituency would leave both cities blank.

    Three counts against the margin

    A seat is counted on the first measure when the names left off its draft exceed its 2022 margin. The second leaves out the names entered as dead. The third also leaves out the names entered as already enrolled at another place, who can still vote there. All three assume that every elector counted would have voted, and voted for the runner-up, which no one can know. None of them allows for turnout, because the compilation's elector figures are not used.

    Against the district

    Ahmedabad and Surat left off far more names than the rest of the state, and the parties did not win evenly across districts. So each seat's rate is also set against the rate of its own district, and those differences are averaged for each party's seats.

    What these tables cannot show

    • Whether women, the old or the young were left off more often. No table published for Gujarat splits anything by sex or age.
    • How many names each constituency put back. The final roll is published by district only.
    • How many names were deleted after a notice and a hearing. The deletions after the draft are given as one figure for each district.
    • Whether each removal was right, or how anyone left off would have voted.
