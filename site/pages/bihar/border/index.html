    Names left off or deleted per 100 electors, by border

    Names left off or deleted for every 100 electors on the 2025 roll, with constituencies grouped by the border their district touches. Seats on two borders form their own group.

    Share of all names left off or deleted

    Each group's share of Bihar's names left off or deleted beside its share of the electorate. Where the first bar is longer, the group lost more names than its size would suggest.

    Who was left off or deleted

    Women's share of the names left off or deleted, and the range for the share of names left off the draft classified Muslim. Religion is guessed from names. The low end leaves out unclassified names and the high end counts all of them as Muslim.

    Phase 2 against phase 1

    Phase 1 is the names left off the draft roll of 1 August 2025, and phase 2 the names deleted after the draft, before the final roll. The chart gives phase 2 as a share of phase 1, so a higher share means more names came off after the draft.

    Click a column to sort.

    The Nepal and Bengal belts, and Seemanchal within them, also send the most migrants out of Bihar, for a season or for good. A migrant who has moved away is rightly marked "shifted". A higher rate on the border fits that explanation as well as any other. The lists show that a name came off the roll, and cannot show which explanation is right.

    A constituency counts as a border seat when its district touches Nepal, West Bengal, Jharkhand or Uttar Pradesh. The shares of Muslim names come from the name classification in the booth lists. The low figure counts only names classified Muslim, and the high figure adds every name left unclassified.
