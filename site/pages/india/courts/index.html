Court cases

    The court cases on the revision, each matter with every entry about it in date order. An entry is in a matter when it names the matter's case number or says it belongs to it. An entry that is only connected to a matter is listed under related. Every entry links to its place on the page of protests and disputes, which quotes its source.

    The matters

    Each matter with its court, the number of entries about it and the next date listed in it, where a source gives one.

      Each matter

      The entries are in English and keep their sources' words. A document is the court's own order or record, and a report is a newspaper's account.

      Other cases in the courts

      Petitions and applications that no entry ties to a matter above, by court, newest first. Entries that describe their case in the same words are kept together.

      How the entries are grouped

      Two entries are put in one matter only where an entry says they are the same case, or that one is heard with, tagged with or connected to the other. A case number joins a matter only through an entry that names it beside a number the matter already has. An entry with no case number is in a matter when it describes itself in the words other entries in the matter use, or when it cites an entry in the matter, or is cited by one, and both are before the same court.

      An entry that names the case numbers of two matters, or that cites an entry in a matter without saying it is the same case, is listed under related and is left out of the matter's count. The parties and bodies listed for a matter are those its entries name. A private person who brought a case is described by role.

      Where a matter holds separate cases on one subject, a note in the matter says so.
