    Did the names left off or deleted change West Bengal's result?

    Five pieces below ask this question, and they reach different answers because they test different things. One sets the names left off or deleted against each seat's margin, one against the swing between elections, one assumes how those electors would have voted, and one recomputes the result with no revision at all. A count of names larger than a margin shows that those names could have decided a seat. It cannot show that they did. Read them side by side.

      What politicians, officials and commentators say, set beside the official figures

      The official figures are counts of what the Election Commission and the Chief Electoral Officers published, and they support no more than those counts allow. Several of the people below go further. Some use data that is not in the official counts, such as the outcomes of West Bengal's adjudication seat by seat, which the Sabar Institute compiled from the named lists. Some compare groups of seats without allowing for the other ways those seats differ. Some draw a conclusion about purpose from a pattern in the figures, which the lists cannot show.

      Each panel quotes the person's own words and says what the quotation rests on. It then sets the official figures beside it and says where those figures stop. Where a person reaches further than the figures, the figures cannot settle the question either way, and the panel says so.
