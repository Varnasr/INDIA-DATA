    Each dot is a rank correlation inside one state, between each district's rate and one thing about the district measured by the National Family Health Survey in 2019-21. A value above zero means the districts with more of that thing lost more names. A value near zero means no relationship.     How the figures were put together

    The district circumstances are from the National Family Health Survey 2019-21, the fifth round, which published a fact sheet for every district. They are taken from a published compilation of those fact sheets, and every Bihar value in it was checked against the Bihar compendium the Ministry of Health published. The survey published its district fact sheets in two phases. The compilation holds the first, which covers Bihar, Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Telangana and West Bengal among the states with district figures here. Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Delhi were in the second phase and are not on this page yet.

    The survey used the districts of 2019 and 2020. A district made after that is counted under the district it was made from, and the districts sharing one survey district are added together before the rate is worked out. Bengaluru's four districts are counted as the survey's Bangalore, Kolkata's two as Kolkata, Vijayanagara under Bellary, and Mulugu and Narayanpet under the districts they came from. Telangana's Warangal and Hanumakonda are matched to the survey's Warangal Rural and Warangal Urban, which they replaced with changed boundaries in 2021.

    Each state is read in its own measure, as on the urban page. In Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Telangana it is the names left off the draft per 100 on the roll before, and Telangana's district figures are the state's as reported in the press. In Bihar it is both phases per 100 on the 2025 roll. West Bengal and Kerala published no removals below the state, so there it is the fall in the roll, which takes in ordinary deaths and moves too. A rank correlation is the ordinary correlation worked out on the districts' ranks, so one very large district cannot drive it. The size it must reach before chance becomes an unlikely explanation is 1.96 divided by the square root of one less than the number of districts. Kerala has 14 districts, so there a correlation must pass 0.54.
