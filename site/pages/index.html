Who was taken off India's electoral rolls

In 2025 and 2026 the Election Commission revised the electoral rolls of most of India house by house, in what it called a Special Intensive Revision. Every elector had to fill in a fresh form, and names were left off the new rolls. This site counts those names from the lists and figures the Commission and the states have published, and sets out who was removed, where, and on what recorded reason.

Names left off the draft rolls, state by state

Each state and union territory is shaded by the names left off its draft roll for every 100 electors on the roll before the revision, as its Chief Electoral Officer published them. Grey means the state was not in the revision or has published no figure. Hover over a state to see its figure, and tap it to open its own analysis, or the All India page where it has none.

How the revision was run

The rules changed while the revision was under way. The Commission issued fresh orders, the Supreme Court made Aadhaar one of the documents an elector could show and, in West Bengal, gave disputed claims to judicial officers, and many states had their dates moved after they were announced. How the revision was run sets out every order and judgment in date order, each linked to its document, and draws every state's schedule as it was set and as it was moved.

Why this site exists

The revision decides who can vote. The lists behind it are public, but they are hard to read. They come as thousands of files, one for each polling part, often scanned, in the language of the state. The totals the Commission announces do not say who was removed, how old they were, or whether the removals in a seat were larger than the margin that decided it.

This site reads those lists and figures and turns them into counts. It shows where removals were heaviest, whether women were removed more than men, and what reason was written against the names. A reason on a list is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it. The counts can show where removals fell and on whom. They cannot show why any one name was removed, or whether that removal was right.

How to read it

  • Only counts are published here. No name, EPIC number or address appears anywhere on this site.
  • Religion and caste are guessed from names, so they are given as ranges.
  • Every page opens with what the data shows and ends with its limits and its own questions. The method pages give every source.
  • The pages can be read in English, Hindi, Urdu, Telugu, Tamil and Bengali from the menu at the top. The translations are awaiting review, and where they differ the English page is correct.
