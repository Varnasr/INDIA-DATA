For journalists

This page is in English only.

This page is for reporters, editors and researchers who want to use a figure, a chart or a table from these pages. It sets out how to cite a page, what may be reused and on what terms, where each figure comes from, the words to use for names left off and names deleted, and the files and feeds that carry the data.

Citing a page

Every page has a box headed "Cite this page" near its foot. It gives the page's title, its address, a code for the version of the page and the date it was read, and a button copies the whole reference. The version code is there because figures change when a final roll or a correction comes in, and a reader of your story can then see exactly which version you used.

A figure quoted from a page should be credited to the source the page names for it, such as a Chief Electoral Officer's table or the Election Commission's affidavit, with these pages as where it was found and counted.

Reusing the charts, maps and tables

The analysis, charts, maps and tables written for these pages may be reused under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. The terms page asks that you credit "Off the Roll (offtheroll.in)" and link to the page you used. Official data keeps the terms of whoever published it, and the terms page lists the maps and typefaces that carry their own licences.

Each chart has buttons beneath it to share it or copy a link to it. A state's overview has a box headed "Embed this chart" with the code to place its chart on another website.

Where a figure comes from

Each figure on a state's pages is taken from a named source, and the page says which. About the data explains how every count is worked out, from the names left off the draft to the fall in the roll and the notices. Each item on the protests and disputes page links the report or order it rests on, and says when a report is for subscribers and only its free part was used.

When a recorded figure is found to be wrong, the change is listed on the corrections page with the date, what the page said before and what it says now. Every addition is listed by date on What's new, and a summary of the week is added at the top of that page every Friday.

The words to use

A name on the roll before the revision whose enumeration form did not come back was left off the draft. A name taken off the roll by an officer's order after the draft, on an objection in Form 7 or on the officer's own motion, was deleted, and that order can be appealed. The two are different counts, and the fall in the roll is a third, the difference between the roll before the revision and the final roll. Words we use sets out each one.

The figures do not show anyone's citizenship, religion or intent, and no such claim is made in these pages except in the words of whoever made it, with their name. States are measured in their own terms and are not ranked against each other, for the reasons given under Per 100 electors, and why states are not ranked.

Data, feeds and calendars

  • All downloads, explained: one workbook for each state and for India, the reports and slides, and what each column holds.
  • The open data index: every published data file, with its address, for anyone who wants to read the files directly.
  • The feed of What's new, for a feed reader.
  • The calendar of the revision: draft rolls, claims and final rolls for every state, with a calendar for each state on its overview.
  • Events and the court calendar on the protests and disputes page, for hearings and gatherings to come.

Only counts

These pages publish counts by state, district, constituency and polling station. They never publish or link a list that names an elector, an EPIC number or an address. Private people in a report, such as a petitioner, an applicant under the RTI Act or a booth level officer, are described by their role. Officials acting in office, politicians, judges, lawyers and public commentators are named.
