Part I: The five constituencies

At a glance

6,62,063electors struck off
40.0%of the pre-revision rolls
5 of 5seats where the names struck off exceed the 2023 margin
3,69,972kept but summoned to a hearing

The Special Intensive Revision, a fresh check of every name on the electoral roll, reached Hyderabad in 2026. The pre-revision rolls of five Old City constituencies, the rolls as they stood before the revision began, held 16,57,207 electors. The September lists struck 6,62,063 of them off, 40.0%. They kept 3,69,972 more on the draft roll, the first published version of the revised roll, only on condition that they attend a hearing. These lists hold every elector, so the rate for any group, the share of that group struck off, is a count over everyone in it and involves no sampling.

A seat's winning margin is the number of votes by which its winner beat the runner-up. Every one of the five lost more names than its 2023 winning margin. In Yakutpura and Malakpet the names struck off exceed every valid vote cast in 2023. Yakutpura was won by 878 votes and lost 1,54,982 names. The heaviest burden fell on the oldest electors and on those who could not be traced to the 2002 roll, the roll of the last intensive revision. 74.0% of electors aged 80 and over were struck off, and electors with no 2002 link were struck off at twice the rate of those with one.

A polling part is the section of a constituency's roll that votes at one polling station, and it is the smallest unit the lists can be added up by. Malakpet's list is read by polling-station name, which gives 60 stations instead of parts, and it is incomplete. It holds 1,42,688 names struck off, while a separate count of the published Malakpet list finds 1,47,799. For the other four constituencies the two counts agree exactly.

Across the five constituencies the names struck off equal 99.0% of the valid votes cast in 2023. In every constituency more names were struck off than the winning candidate received.

No election has been held on the revised roll, so no result has been changed. Against the 2023 margins, only Yakutpura is close. There the runner-up would need 50.8% of the removed electors who would vote, at 2023 turnout and leaving out those entered as dead, to overturn a margin of 878. In Charminar and Malakpet the runner-up would need 73.6% to 83.0%, and in Chandrayangutta and Bahadurpura no split of the removed would be enough. Muslim names are a smaller share of those struck off than of the roll in all five seats, by 2.6 to 10.4 points, so where Muslim electors lean to AIMIM, the removals take fewer of its voters than of its opponents'.

The reasons recorded

The list of the absent, shifted and dead, known as the ASDD list, holds every elector struck off and the reason an officer wrote against each name. 64.2% of removals were entered as "permanently shifted", 20.8% as "untraceable or absent", 8.7% as dead and 6.0% as enrolled elsewhere. Each is what the officer wrote down after a visit, or after failing to find the elector, and nobody checked it.

Age

In every constituency, the older the elector, the likelier the removal. Between 70.1% and 76.6% of electors aged 80 and over were struck off, and 19.3% to 25.6% of those aged 18 to 24. Death explains part of the removals at the oldest ages, and 57,526 deaths were recorded. But the rate roughly doubles between ages 18 to 24 and 30 to 39, when almost nobody dies, so death does not explain it all.

Women and men

Women and men were struck off at similar rates, 39.9% of women and 40.5% of men. Inside the same polling part the gap runs from -2.2 to 2.4 points, one way in some constituencies and the other way in others.

Religion

The roll does not record religion, so it is guessed from each elector's name, and a separate classification agrees on 99% of names. Across the five constituencies 52.0% of Hindu-name electors were struck off and 35.3% of Muslim-name electors. Inside the same polling part, where the booth officer, the street and the housing are the same for everyone, Hindu-name electors were struck off 9.6 to 24.5 points more often, in 728 of 823 parts large enough to compare. The lists give religion and age separately and never together, so part of this gap may be a gap by age.

The 2002 roll

The revision asks each elector to show a link to the roll of the 2002 intensive revision, the last one held, through their own name or a parent's. Where the lists record it, 51.6% of electors with no link were struck off against 25.3% of those with one. Inside the same polling part unlinked electors were struck off 21 to 28 points more often, in 1,024 of 1,098 parts. Anyone who moved to the city after 2002, or whose family name is spelt differently on the old roll, starts at a disadvantage. Malakpet's list carries no linkage.

Hearings

3,69,972 electors kept on the draft roll were summoned to a hearing, 22.3% of electors on the pre-revision rolls. Each case was listed for a hearing slot, a set date and time at which a batch of cases is called, and there were 7,164 slots. Each summons rests on one or more rules, set out in the table below. Not being traceable to the last intensive revision, the no-mapping rule, was the commonest, 31.6% of flags. The others flag a name that does not match the old roll, six or more children recorded against one parent, or family ages that look impossible, such as a parent less than 15 years older than a child. The heaviest single slot listed 459 cases, in Charminar, where the typical slot listed 38. The lists do not record how long a slot lasted.

Against Hyderabad and Telangana

On the state's draft-roll figures, the five Old City seats lost 39.9% of their roll and Hyderabad's other ten seats 41.6%. Every seat in the district lost between 34.6% and 45.2%, and 2 seats outside the Old City lost more than any Old City seat. Across Hyderabad district the draft left off 41.0%, and in the rest of Telangana 18.6%. The removals are heavy across the whole capital region.

Key insights

  • 6,62,063 electors were struck off, 40.0% of the five pre-revision rolls, and 3,69,972 more were summoned to a hearing.
  • More names were struck off than the 2023 margin in all 5 constituencies, and more than all the valid votes cast in Yakutpura and Malakpet.
  • Yakutpura was won by 878 votes and lost 1,54,982 names, 176 times the margin.
  • The rest of the city lost as large a share. On the state's figures the five Old City seats lost 39.9% of their roll, Hyderabad's other ten 41.6%, and the rest of Telangana 18.6%.
  • 74.0% of electors aged 80 and over were struck off, against 20.9% of those aged 18 to 24.
  • Electors with no link to the 2002 roll were about twice as likely to be struck off, 51.6% against 25.3%.
  • 20.8% of removals were entered as "untraceable or absent", ranging from 6.5% to 38.3% between constituencies.
  • 459 cases were listed for a single hearing slot in Charminar.
  • 30 polling parts lost more than 60% of their roll.

What this shows and what it does not

The lists show how many electors were struck off, from which parts, and who they were by age, sex, religion guessed from names, and the link to the 2002 roll. They do not show whether any individual removal was right. A reason code cannot settle that. A household that moved out of the Old City is rightly marked "shifted", and the files cannot tell it apart from an elector who was at home when the officer missed them. Religion and age are counted separately, so a gap by religion may partly be a gap by age. The Malakpet list used here is incomplete. These are the September lists. On 7 September the Commission extended the calendar, with hearings to 5 November and the final roll on 9 November, so names put back after hearings are not in these counts.

Conclusion and what the Election Commission should do

In five constituencies the revision struck off 40.0% of the electors on the rolls, more names than the margin that decided every one of the five seats in 2023, and in two of them more than every valid vote cast. The burden fell on the old, and on those the process could not trace to a roll prepared 24 years earlier. The final roll has not yet been published, and the hearings are still being held.

What the Election Commission should do before the final roll:

  1. Record and publish the basis for every removal entered as "untraceable or absent", 1,37,825 names in all, including the visits made and the notice given.
  2. Publish how removals of electors with no link to the 2002 roll were decided, since the link is the revision's own test and the gap it produces is the largest in the files.
  3. Schedule hearings so that each case can be heard. Some slots listed several hundred cases.
  4. Explain the removals in the 30 parts that lost more than 60% of their roll.
  5. Publish restorations after hearings, part by part, so that the final count can be set against these lists.

Method and sources

  • The rolls before the revision, the list of the absent, shifted and dead, and the discrepancy and no-mapping lists come from the Chief Electoral Officer, Telangana, the Election Commission's officer in charge of the state's rolls, for the five constituencies, September 2026, added up by polling part. The elector-by-elector lists were compiled for these five seats only.
  • Religion is guessed from each elector's name because the roll does not record it, and a separate classification agrees on 99% of names.
  • Hearing schedules come from the same lists, 28 August to 12 October 2026.
  • 2023 Assembly result: Election Commission of India.

The deletion rate is the electors on the list of the absent, shifted and dead divided by the electors on the roll before the revision, for the group in question. The within-part difference is the rate of one group minus the other inside each part, pooled across parts with at least 20 of each and weighted by the smaller group. Part counts add up exactly to the constituency totals. No name, EPIC number or address is published.
