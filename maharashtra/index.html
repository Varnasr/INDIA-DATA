Maharashtra 2026

Overview

    This page is in English only for now. Every figure on it comes from the Election Commission or the Chief Electoral Officer, and where it reached us through the press, the page says so.

    Find a district

    Type a district's name and its figures appear below with a link you can send. The revision's figures were published by district only, so this page has no assembly constituencies to find.

    Each district is shaded by the names left off its draft roll for every 100 electors on its roll before the revision. Tap a district for its figures. District boundaries drawn from DataMeet's assembly constituency map (CC BY 2.5 India), simplified, with each constituency placed in its district as the Election Commission lists it.

    The roll, step by step

    Before the draft, every elector on the roll was given an enumeration form, a short form to fill in and hand back to the official for the polling station. The draft roll is the list published once the forms were collected, and a name whose form did not come back was left off it. Maharashtra's draft came out on 31 August 2026. Electors can now ask to be added or object to names on it, and only after that comes the final roll. The bars start above zero so that the changes can be seen.

    Maharashtra's dates, as they were set and as they were moved, are drawn beside every other state's on How the revision was run.

    Per 100 electors, by district

    The names left off each district's draft roll, for every 100 electors on its roll before the revision. Counting per 100 electors puts large and small districts on one scale, so a rate of 20 means one elector in five was left off. The figures are from the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO), who keeps the state's rolls for the Election Commission. Mumbai City, Mumbai Suburban, Thane, Palghar and Pune are in red, and the upright line marks the rate for the state as a whole.

    The reasons

    The CEO's press note lists five reasons a form was not collected. The elector was absent or could not be found, had shifted, had died, was enrolled at another place, or the form was not collected for some other reason, including a refusal to fill it in. The note gives no count for any of them, for the state or for a district. The counts below were reported in the press, rounded to the nearest thousand, and are given in lakh (one lakh is 100,000). A reason is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it.

    Mumbai and Pune

    Mumbai City, Mumbai Suburban, Thane, Palghar and Pune are the five districts that hold the cities of Mumbai and Pune and the towns that have grown round them. Here they are set against the other 31 districts.

    Each of the five districts' share of the state's roll before the revision, and its share of the names left off. Where the second dot sits to the right of the first, the district lost more than its share.

    Women

    The CEO published the draft roll by sex and nothing else by sex. The roll before the revision and the names left off are given as totals only, so the names left off cannot be counted by sex. What can be shown is women's share of each district's draft roll. Each dot is one district, placed by how far its share sits above or below the state's. Hover or tap a dot for its name.

    Notices

    An elector who stays on the draft roll can still be sent a notice to prove the entry, with documents, at a hearing. The CEO's press note says notices go to electors who could not be linked to the rolls of the last intensive revision, in 2002 to 2004, and to entries whose details do not agree, and gives no number for either.
