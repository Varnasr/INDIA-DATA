Gender, religion, caste

The roll records sex. It records neither religion nor caste, so those two are read from names, and a name is weaker evidence than a record. This page says how much weaker, sets each estimate beside a population baseline, and gives a range wherever the names cannot decide.

Religion

The booth files classify each removed elector's name as Hindu, Muslim, or unclassified. The unclassified could fall either way, so the Muslim share is a range: from counting none of them to counting all of them.

Muslim share of removals against the 2011 census, by district

The bar spans the range from name classification; the dot is the district's Muslim share of population in 2011. Where the dot sits inside the bar, the removals are consistent with the population.

The 2011 census is fifteen years old, and Bihar's Muslim share has risen since (17.70% in the 2023 caste survey against 16.87% in 2011). A baseline that is too low makes removals look heavier than they are. Name classification is also least reliable exactly where it matters, among names shared across communities.

Caste

The earlier dashboard reported that 60.9% of classified deletions were EBC. That figure rested on one entry: the surname mapping filed Devi, the title most married women carry in place of a surname, under Lohar (EBC). Devi is the commonest "surname" among the removed, 19.8 lakh of them, so two million women were counted as EBC. Its district caste charts were not measurements either: every district carried the same five shares, the 2023 state survey's, multiplied by its deletions. Both are replaced below.

What the files do hold is the surname of each removed elector and of their guardian (father or husband). The guardian's surname is far more informative, since women's own entries are mostly Devi, Kumari or Khatun. Both are classified two ways: A with the project's own surname mapping, reached through a spelling bridge from the romanised Hindi of the lists; B strictly, counting only surnames that belong almost entirely to one group and setting names like Singh, Prasad, Ray, Mahato, Mandal, Sharma, Thakur, Chaudhary and Manjhi aside as spanning groups.

The 25 commonest surnames among the removed

Why no caste can be called over- or under-represented yet. A share among the removed means something only against the same share among all electors, surname by surname. These files hold surnames of the removed alone. With surname counts from the full draft roll, even for a sample of booths, each surname would have its own deletion rate, as each age and religion does in the Hyderabad analysis, and the caste question could be answered rather than bounded.

Caste survey baseline: the Bihar caste-based survey released on 2 October 2023 (population 13,07,25,310: EBC 36.01%, BC 27.12%, SC 19.65%, ST 1.68%, unreserved 15.52%; Muslims 17.70%). Muslim castes listed within EBC (10.29 points), BC (2.09) and unreserved (4.80) are moved to the Muslim group, so the Hindu-caste shares are EBC 25.72, BC 25.03 and General 10.72. The strict classification and the basis for each surname are in surname_classes.csv; the project mapping is in the raw data folder.
