    Women's share of each reason, by age

    Every name left off the draft in phase 1 has one of four reasons against it, which is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it. For each reason and age group, the chart shows the share of those left off who were women. At 50% women and men would be left off equally.

    Two reasons, set against the records

    Two of the reasons can be checked against other records. The names entered as dead are compared with the number of adults who die in Bihar in a year, from the Sample Registration System for 2024. The excess of young women marked shifted is compared with the women who moved on marriage, from the 2011 census.

    Young women marked shifted, against marriage movement, by district

    Each dot is a district. It sets women's share of those aged 18 to 29 marked shifted against the share of the district's women who had moved on marriage in the ten years before the 2011 census. The rank correlation below compares the order of the districts on the two measures. It runs from −1 to 1, and near 0 means no link.

    Religion

    The roll does not record religion, so it is guessed from names. The booth lists classify the name of each elector left off the draft as Hindu, Muslim or unclassified. An unclassified name could be either, so the Muslim share is given as a range, from counting none of them as Muslim to counting all of them.

    Muslim share of names left off the draft against the 2011 census, by district

    The bar is the range guessed from names. The dark dot is the district's Muslim share of the population in 2011, and the open dot is that share carried forward to 2025. Where a dot sits inside the bar, the names left off the draft are in line with the population.

    The 2011 census is the most recent count of religion by district. The 2025 figures carry each district's 2011 share forward at the growth rates of its Muslim and total populations between the 2001 and 2011 censuses. Jehanabad and Arwal were one district in 2001, so both take their joint rate.

    Caste

    The roll does not record caste. The lists give a surname for each elector left off the draft and for the father or husband named beside them. The father's or husband's surname tells more, because most women are listed as Devi, Kumari or Khatun, which say nothing about caste. Surnames are classified in two ways. Classification A is a broad mapping of surnames to castes. Classification B is strict and counts only surnames that belong almost entirely to one group, setting aside names such as Singh, Prasad, Ray, Mahato, Mandal, Sharma, Thakur, Chaudhary and Manjhi, which several groups carry. Only findings that hold under both are reported.

    The 25 commonest surnames among the names left off the draft

    These are shares of the names left off the draft. To turn them into rates, the same surnames would have to be counted on the draft roll. Even a sample of booths would give each caste group its own rate, the share of its electors who were left off the draft.

    The caste baseline is the Bihar caste-based survey released on 2 October 2023. It counted 13,07,25,310 people, of whom 36.01% were EBC, 27.12% BC, 19.65% SC, 1.68% ST and 15.52% unreserved, with Muslims at 17.70%. Muslim castes listed under EBC (10.29 points), BC (2.09) and unreserved (4.80) are moved to the Muslim group here, which leaves the Hindu castes at EBC 25.72, BC 25.03 and General 10.72. The strict classification gives a surname to a group only if almost everyone carrying it belongs to that group.
