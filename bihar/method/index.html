    How the lists are counted

    Booths

    The phase 1 lists cover 77,837 booths and 65,59,626 names left off the draft, which is the Chief Electoral Officer's total.

    Party names in the booth results

    Votes added up from the booths match the declared 2024 result in every Lok Sabha seat. In nine seats the booth results gave a party name that differs from the declared result, and the evidence for each correction is below. With those corrected, the first and second parties in all 40 seats are the declared winner and runner-up.

    Titles are not surnames

    Most women carry Devi, Kumari or Khatun in place of a surname, and none of the three says anything about caste. They are set aside, and caste is read first from the surname of the father or husband.

    Two classifications of surnames

    Caste is read from surnames in two ways, set out on the People page. The dataset of official caste lists by Jolad, Kalyani and Baharadia of FLAME University, published on Harvard Dataverse (doi:10.7910/DVN/WT5VIY) under a CC0 licence, is the source for the classification of a few surnames, from the Bihar lists of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes.

    Reasons are read as entered

    Each name on the phase 1 lists has the reason the officer gave for leaving it off the draft. That reason is what the officer wrote down, and nobody checked it. The four reasons are counted as written and none is regrouped. The counts agree with the booth list in all 77,837 booths, and the counts of women and men agree with the Chief Electoral Officer's phase 1 figures in every constituency. The phase 2 lists give no reasons.

    Seats that share a name

    Bihar has two seats called Kalyanpur (16 and 131) and two called Pipra (17 and 42), and the constituency number tells them apart. Minapur (90) belongs to the Vaishali Lok Sabha seat.

    Border constituencies

    A constituency counts as a border constituency when its district touches Nepal, West Bengal, Jharkhand or Uttar Pradesh.

    Research questions

    What this shows and what it does not

    The lists show how many names were left off the draft or deleted after it and from which booths, and the sex and name of each person. That is enough to set those names against the margins that decided each seat. They also record the reason the officer gave for each name left off the draft in phase 1. They cannot show whether that reason was true, whether the name was rightly left off, or how the elector voted. Religion and caste are guessed from names. Questions and answers.

    Left off the draft means named on the phase 1 lists of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR), of names on the roll before the revision that were not carried into the draft roll. Deleted after the draft means named on the phase 2 lists, of names taken off between the draft and the final roll. The draft roll of 1 August 2025 is the roll after phase 1, open to claims and objections, and the final roll of 30 September 2025 is the roll after they were heard. Per 100 electors is the names left off or deleted across both phases divided by the constituency's 2025 electorate. A winning margin is the winner's votes less the runner-up's. A rank correlation compares the order of districts or seats on two measures, from −1 to 1, and one under 0.2 either way is read as no relationship. The booth leader is the party with the most votes in the booth in 2024, and the segment leader the party with the most votes in the assembly segment in 2024.

    Only counts are published, never a name. For analysis that looks at named voters, see the Sabar Institute.
