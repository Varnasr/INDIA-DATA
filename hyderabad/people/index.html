Age, sex, religion and the 2002 roll

The Old City files cover every elector, so each group has a true deletion rate: the share of that group on the pre-revision roll who were struck off. And because groups live side by side in the same polling parts, each gap can be tested inside the part, where the officer, the locality and the housing are shared.

The same gap, inside each part

Hindu-name rate minus Muslim-name rate, pooled across parts with at least 20 of each, each part weighted by its smaller group.

Two things this cannot separate. Age: the files give religion and age separately, not together, so if Hindu-name electors in these wards are older, part of the gap is age. And movement: a Hindu-name household that has left the Old City is a legitimate "shifted" deletion. The within-part comparison removes differences between localities, not differences between households.

Set beside Bihar

In Bihar women were over-represented among the removed in every district; here sex barely separates. In Bihar the Muslim-name share of removals is a range that brackets the census in most of Seemanchal and exceeds it in south and central Bihar; here, with every elector counted, Hindu-name electors were struck off more often. The designs differ (why), and so do the places: an old urban core with heavy movement, against a largely rural state whose workforce leaves for months at a time.

Religion: a name-based estimate that agrees 99% with an independent rebuild; the roll records none. Electors whose names were classified as neither are left out of both religion rates. 2002 linkage: the elector maps to the 2002 intensive revision roll in the export.
