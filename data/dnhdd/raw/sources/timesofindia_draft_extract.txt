NEW DELHI: The Union territory of Daman and Diu and Dadra and Nagar Haveli witnessed the deletion of 29.7% electors -- including a whopping 22.5% on account of being absent or untraceable -- in its draft roll published on Friday, a trend linked by SIR-related officials to the UT's large migrant population and highly industrialised status. Of the 4.28 lakh electorate of the UT on the day of announcement of SIR on June 4, 2026, barely a little over 3 lakh, or 70.3% turned in their enumeration forms. The remaining 29.7% were struck off the roll, 1.7% electors were found to have died, 4.6% permanently shifted, 0.6% registered at multiple places and 0.2% classified in the 'others' category. The most interesting figure, of course, was the 22.5% electors who had to be marked untraceable or absent as they were not found on their registered addresses despite multiple visits by the booth level officer (BLO). \"The latest door-to-door survey by the health department had put the UT's population at 6.8 lakh, of which around 3 lakh were migrants, largely from states like Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Odisha. They come here for short-term work in the nearly 7,000 industries that make the UT a highly-industrialised zone. These migrants are usually on the move and do not stay here for long periods,\" an officer involved in SIR told TOI.In an official statement, the chief electoral officer attributed the 29.7% deletions to these electors having enrolled in other states/UTs, not being found in existence, not having submitted their forms till July 3 or being reluctant to register as an elector for some reason or the other. While 29.7% is the highest rate of deletion seen so far in any state/UT at the draft roll stage, it is not the final figure, as deleted names can be added back during the claims and objections period from July 10 to Aug 9. Also, fresh enrolments (through Form 6) will also lead to some additions to the roll.
