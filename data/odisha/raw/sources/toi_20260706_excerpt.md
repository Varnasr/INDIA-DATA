# Times of India, 6 July 2026 (read with Firecrawl 2026-10-06), excerpt

https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bhubaneswar/draft-electoral-roll-ganjam-tops-deletions-malkangiri-sees-sharpest-decline-at-10-25/articleshow/132217485.cms

Bhubaneswar: Ganjam saw the highest number of names deleted during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls with Malkangiri recording the steepest proportional decline, its electorate shrinking by 10.25%, as per the draft roll published by the office of the chief electoral officer (CEO), [Odisha](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/topic/odisha), on Sunday.Out of every 10 electors, Malkangiri lost more than one, with its electorate dropping from 4.62 lakh in the pre-SIR roll to 4.15 lakh in the draft, the sharpest percentage fall among all districts in Odisha.Election Commission authorities said the deletions were based on field verification and prescribed procedures. Citizens whose names are missing from the draft roll can file claims and objections by Aug 4, with the date of publication of the final electoral roll scheduled on Sept 6, Odisha CEO R Santhanagopalan said.The draft electoral roll showed a total reduction of 20.12 lakh electors compared with the pre-SIR roll, which had 3.33 crore electors. The draft roll has 3.13 crore electors.A comparison of district-wise elector data shows Ganjam registered the highest reduction in absolute numbers. The district’s electorate fell from 29.99 lakh before the revision to 27.92 lakh in the draft roll, a decline of 2.07 lakh electors.
Ganjam is followed by Cuttack with a reduction of 1.55 lakh voters, Mayurbhanj (1.11 lakh), Jajpur (1.05 lakh) and Balangir (99,258).Malkangiri recorded the highest decline in the percentage of electors. The district lost 47,355 electors, translating into a 10.25% reduction in its elector base. Cuttack followed with a 7.44% decline, while Balangir recorded a 7.39% reduction.The reduction in voter numbers has become a major political issue, with opposition parties questioning the scale of deletions and sought greater transparency in the revision process. Migration-prone districts such as Malkangiri, Gajapati, Kandhamal, Ganjam and Balangir have reported some of the sharpest percentage declines, raising concerns about the impact on migrant workers and remote tribal populations.
