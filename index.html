The Special Intensive Revision: who was struck off the rolls

In 2025 the Election Commission began re-verifying every elector, state by state, instead of revising the existing rolls. These pages follow two of those revisions from the Commission's own lists: Bihar, the first state to go through it, and five constituencies in Hyderabad's Old City. No names or EPIC numbers appear anywhere on this site.

Two revisions, two kinds of evidence

The two analyses cannot be read against each other number for number, and the reason is the data rather than the places.

Hyderabad: every elector

The Old City exports list each elector on the pre-revision roll with a fate. That gives a true deletion rate for any group the file describes: by age, sex, name-estimated religion, and whether the elector links to the 2002 roll. It is the stronger design.

Bihar: the deleted, by booth

Bihar's evidence is the list of those removed from the draft roll, booth by booth, with the booth's 2024 Lok Sabha result from Form 20. The count per booth and per constituency is solid. Composition is not: without the full roll, the share of a group among the deleted cannot be set against its share among electors except through a census baseline, and the Bihar pages say so wherever it matters.

How the Bihar figures were rebuilt

The earlier Bihar dashboard at this address reported 39.2 million deletions "in 2020" falling to 6.56 million "in 2024", and an NDA-INDIA comparison that ran the wrong way. Both came from how the booth files were joined, not from the rolls: each booth's deletions were counted about six times, and the 2024 party labels were wrong in nine seats. The figures now on these pages are rebuilt from the booth files, reconcile with the Commission's totals, and match the declared 2024 result in all 40 seats. The method page sets out what changed and why.
