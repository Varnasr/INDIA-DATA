Karnataka 2026

Overview

    Find a district

    The revision was published by district only, so each district is shaded by the names left off the draft roll for every 100 electors on the roll of 16 June. The draft roll is the roll the revision published once those names had been taken off, and counting per 100 electors lets a small district be set beside a large one. Type a district's name, and its figures appear below with a link you can send.

    District boundaries drawn from DataMeet's assembly constituency map (CC BY 2.5 India), simplified, with each constituency placed in its election district as the Election Commission lists it.

    The roll, step by step

    The state's roll, step by step, from 16 June to 25 September 2026. After the draft, people could file claims to be added and objections to names on it. By 25 September these had closed, and the notices sent to electors were still being heard. The bars start above zero so that the changes can be seen, and the figure on each bar is the count. The final roll is due on 27 October 2026.

    Karnataka's dates, as they were set and as they were moved, are drawn beside every other state's on How the revision was run.

    Per 100 electors, by district

    Names left off the draft roll of 24 August 2026, for every 100 electors on the roll of 16 June 2026, in each of the 34 election districts of the Chief Electoral Officer, the official in charge of the state's electoral roll. Bengaluru's four districts are marked in red.

    The reasons, district by district

    Each district's removals split by the reason entered against the name. Districts are in the same order as above.

    Bengaluru

    Bangalore Urban and the three B.B.M.P districts, set against the rest of the state.

    Women

    The roll of 16 June is published as a total only and the names left off are not split by sex, so women's share of the removals cannot be counted from the Chief Electoral Officer's tables.

    After the draft

    Each small chart follows one district's roll from 16 June to the draft and to 25 September, with 16 June set at 100. The dashed line is the June level. Bengaluru's four districts are in red. A rise after the draft counts new voters as well as names put back, so it is the most that could have been restored.
