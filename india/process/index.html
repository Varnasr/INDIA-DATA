    What made it harder to stay on the roll

    What an elector had to do, or get past, to stay on the roll or get back on it. Each point is taken from an order, a court record or an official figure, and links to it.

        The census beside the summary revision

        Form 6, Form 7 and the declaration

        Form 6 is how a new elector, or anyone left off, asks to be put on the roll. Form 7 is how anyone objects to a name already on it. During the revision the Commission added a declaration to Form 6 and changed it twice. This is what each version asked for, and what the orders and the Supreme Court said about the forms.

          Claims and objections after the draft, state by state

          Names left off when the draft roll was published, set against names added and deleted between the draft and the final roll. Additions include people enrolling for the first time, so they are the most that could have been put back, and fewer than that in fact.

          Bulk Form 7 filings reported, state by state

          Each row in the first part of the table is one report of Form 7 objections filed in numbers at one place or by one filer. The count is the one the report gives, of forms or of the electors objected to, and it is left blank where the report gives none. Who filed is given by role or party as the report gives it, and where a party made the allegation the row says so. The second part holds Form 7s generated in numbers in registration officers' names, which the officers or the Chief Electoral Officer said the Commission's software produced when an elector was not approved at a hearing. No person filed those as an objector. Every row links to the reports behind it on the protests and disputes page, where the source is quoted.

          Notices: the no-mapping list and the discrepancy list

          Electors who stayed on the draft roll could still be sent a notice to prove their entry. Two lists produced the notices. No mapping means the elector could not be linked to the roll of the last intensive revision, 2002 to 2006 depending on the state. The logical discrepancy list is the Commission's list of mismatches in names, ages and family links, such as a parent fewer than 15 years older than the child. An elector can be on both lists. The chart sets each state's notices against its draft roll. Dark dots rest on a Chief Electoral Officer's or a court's document, light ones on a press report quoting the Chief Electoral Officer.

          The hearings, state by state

          An elector sent a notice is called to a hearing, and from 26 September 2026 documents could also be collected at home. The officer then decides whether the name stays on the roll. The table gives each state's notices sent, its latest count of hearings held and its latest count of names deleted after a notice, each with its date and its source. The chart follows the states that published these counts on three dates or more. A filled point rests on a Chief Electoral Officer's document and a hollow one on a press report. The dashed line is the notices sent.
