Part I: All India

At a glance

13.39 croreleft off the draft rolls
71.4%shifted, absent or other
21.0%dead
14.1removed per 100 electors

The Special Intensive Revision has been carried out in three phases: Bihar in 2025, twelve states and union territories from November 2025, and eighteen more in 2026. In the 31 that have published a draft roll, 13,39,21,374 electors on the rolls before the revision were not carried onto it: 14.1 for every 100. Tripura, the last, publishes its draft in December.

Every state gave a reason for most of its removals. Of the 13.27 crore names it split by reason, 2.78 crore (21.0%) were entered as dead, 9.48 crore (71.4%) as shifted, absent or other, and 1.01 crore (7.6%) as enrolled more than once. Deaths are about one in five. The other four in five rest on an officer's entry that the elector was not where the roll placed them, or was on it twice.

A reason is what an officer entered after a visit or its absence. It is not a finding. The figures cannot say how many entries were wrong.

The three reasons, by phase

The share entered as dead fell from 34.0% in Bihar to 22.2% in phase 2 and 18.1% in phase 3, while the share entered as shifted, absent or other rose from 55.3% to 71.0% and 73.6%. Removals per 100 electors rose with each phase: 8.3, 12.9 and 17.0.

State by state

Among states that removed ten lakh names or more, the share entered as shifted, absent or other runs from 49.7% in Andhra Pradesh to 91.1% in Delhi. Among them the share entered as dead is highest in Odisha (41.7%), and the share entered as enrolled more than once in Andhra Pradesh (16.4%). One revision under one set of instructions produced very different mixes of reasons from state to state, and the Commission has not explained why.

Removals per 100 electors range from 2.5 in Lakshadweep to 32.8 in Delhi. Uttar Pradesh removed the most names in all, 2.89 crore.

Women among the removed

Only ten states have published their removals by sex, or their roll by sex before the revision and on the draft. In 7 of the ten, women are a larger share of the names removed than of the roll, by up to 7.6 points in Bihar. Tamil Nadu and Karnataka are within a point of even, and Delhi runs the other way, by 4.1 points. Most phase 3 states have published no split by sex, so this is not the national picture.

Across all states the removal rate goes most with the share of residents born in another state (0.53), but that does not hold within phase 2 (0.04); it comes from phase 3 (0.68). In phase 3, income per head, urban share, Muslim share, sex ratio and share of residents born in another state are all related to the removal rate, because the large destination states of phase 3 are richer, more urban and home to more migrants than the North-Eastern states revised with them, and 15 states cannot separate one of these from another. The Muslim share of a state's population shows no consistent relationship: 0.12 across all states, -0.34 in phase 2 and 0.53 in phase 3. Among the larger states, the share entered as enrolled more than once goes most with literacy (-0.78).

States on an international border removed fewer names, not more: a median of 8.3 per 100 electors across 13 border states, against 15.2 across 17 interior states. None of these relationships says who was removed within a state; a state-level relationship is not a finding about individuals.

After the draft roll

After the draft rolls, 17 states have published how many more names they removed at hearings and on objections: 27.6 lakh in all. No state has said why, so these removals are outside every figure by reason in this report. Additions after the draft are outside it too; the final rolls of twelve phase 3 states are still to come.

In five Old City constituencies of Hyderabad the September lists hold every elector, not only the removed, so each group has a true rate of removal. The full report is published separately.

  • Reasons entered: permanently shifted 64.2%, untraceable or absent 20.8%, dead 8.7%, already enrolled 6.0%.

  • No link to the 2002 roll roughly doubled the chance of being struck off: 51.6% against 25.3%.

What the Election Commission should do

13.39 crore names came off the draft rolls, about four in five of them on an entry that the elector had moved, could not be found, or was on the roll twice. The Election Commission should:

  1. Publish a state-by-state table of its own, with the reason for every removal at the draft and after it, exact rather than rounded.
  2. Publish the deletion lists with the reason against each name for every state, as it did for Bihar, so that removals can be counted by booth, sex and age.
  3. Publish the basis for entries marked absent and enrolled more than once.
  4. Publish the outcome of every claim and objection, so that restorations can be set against removals.
  5. Explain why the mix of reasons differs so widely between states under one set of instructions.

Method and sources

The All India figures are the Chief Electoral Officers' own, published when each draft roll came out: from their press notes where those could be read (Lakshadweep, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur and Nagaland), and otherwise as reported in the press. Bihar's row is counted from the deletion lists. The reasons are grouped into the three every state reported. The state rows add to 13.39 crore removed, against the about 13.3 crore reported nationally on 30 September 2026; the difference is rounding. Each row's source is listed on the method page of the site and in the downloadable table.

Parts II and III are counted from the lists themselves; their methods are set out in their own reports. No name, EPIC number or address is published.
