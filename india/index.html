All India

Overview

    Removals per 100 electors, state by state

    One tile for each state and union territory, placed roughly where it lies. The tiles are all the same size, so the map shows neither borders nor area. A state's rate is the names left off its draft roll for every 100 electors on its roll before the revision. Grey tiles have no rate. Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh and Assam were not part of the revision, and Tripura has no draft roll yet.

    The rolls against the adult population

    Each state's roll before the revision, and its draft roll, as a share of its projected population aged 18 and over on 1 March 2026. A roll above 100% holds more names than there are adults. That is what a roll would look like if it still carried people who had died or moved away. Projections by age exist for 19 states.

    Removals against the 2024 vote

    Names left off each state's draft roll, as a share of the votes polled in that state in the 2024 Lok Sabha election.

    The three reasons, by phase

    Where the removed names went, by phase and reason

    Each band is the names one phase removed for one reason, drawn to scale. Phase 1 is Bihar (2025), phase 2 the twelve states and union territories ordered in October 2025, with house visits from November, and phase 3 the eighteen revised in 2026. "Not split" counts the names that a state's published reasons leave unexplained. In a few states the reasons add up to slightly more than the total, so the bands come to a little more than the names removed.

    Removed per 100 electors, every state by phase

    Each dot is a state or union territory. Hover or tap a dot for its name and rate.

    The reasons, state by state

    Each state's removals split by the reason entered, as a share of the removals it has split. Every state reports the same three reasons. They are dead, shifted, absent or other, and enrolled more than once. States are ordered by the share entered as shifted, absent or other.

    Women among the removed

    Only some states have published their removals by sex, or their roll by sex before and after the draft. For each of them, women's share of the names removed beside women's share of the roll.

    What goes with the removal rate

    Each state's removals per 100 electors, and the share of its removals entered as dead or as enrolled more than once, against its characteristics. The figures are rank correlations across states: 1 means the two rise together, 0 no relationship, and anything under 0.2 either way is read as none. Each characteristic's source is shown beside it. Migration is from the 2011 census because no newer figure is published by state. Everything else is from 2019 to 2026.

    Removal rate against the share of residents born in another state

    Each dot is a state, coloured by phase.

    After the final rolls

    Each state's final roll compared with its draft. The draft roll was published first. People could then file claims to be put on it and objections to names on it, and the final roll came after those were decided. The grey bar is the names left off the draft roll for every 100 electors on the roll before the revision. The blue bar is the fall that remains once the final roll is out, after the names added and removed on claims and objections. A state appears once its final roll is published.

    One state's roll, step by step

    Each bar is one step from the roll before the revision to the final roll. The first steps are the reasons for removal at the draft, and the last are the names added and removed on claims and objections. The bars start above zero so that the changes can be seen, and the figure on each bar is the count. Where a state published only its draft and final rolls, the change after the draft is one step.

    Every state and union territory

    Figures at the draft roll. Rounded means the state published its figures in lakh or crore, Short that the reasons published leave some names unexplained, and None that no split by reason was found. The source for each row is on the method page.
