Where the revision stands

    This page is in English only for now. Every figure on it comes from the Election Commission or the Chief Electoral Officer, and where it reached us through the press, the page says so.

    How a revision moves

    Each state goes through the same five steps. Booth level officers first visit every household and collect an enumeration form for each elector, the stage called enumeration. The names of electors whose forms came back make up the draft roll, and the names that did not are left off it. Anyone can then claim to be added (Form 6) or object to a name (Form 7) during the claims and objections period, while electors the Commission could not link to an older roll are sent notices and heard. The final roll closes the revision. A state's stage below is worked out from the latest schedule we hold and from the rolls each Chief Electoral Officer has actually published, as of .

    Every state on one calendar

    One row for each state or union territory, on its latest schedule. The navy bar is the house-to-house visits, the dot the draft roll, the amber bar the claims and objections period and the red diamond the final roll. Where a final roll has been published, the diamond is drawn on the day it came out. The vertical line marks the date this page was last checked against the documents. Hover over or tap a row for its dates.

    How long each revision runs

    Days from the first house visit to the final roll, longest first, for every state that has published a draft roll. A navy dot is a final roll already published, counted to the day it came out. An amber dot is a final roll still to come, counted to the date now set for it, and a red one is a final roll whose date has passed without one that we could find.
