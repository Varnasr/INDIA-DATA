Sources and method

The Election Commission has published no state-by-state table of the reasons for removal. Each state's Chief Electoral Officer announced its own figures when the draft roll was published. This page says where each row of the All India table comes from, and how far each can be trusted.

What is counted

Names left off the draft roll

Each state's figure is the number of electors on the roll before the revision who were not carried onto the draft roll at the end of enumeration, with the reason the booth officer entered for each. That is the stage at which every state published a split by reason. Names removed later, at hearings and on objections before the final roll, are counted separately, because no state has said why they were removed.

Three groups of reasons

The enumeration form carried five reasons: dead, permanently shifted, absent or untraceable, already enrolled elsewhere, and others (including refusals). States reported them differently. Bihar's phase 1 used three categories, with shifted and absent as one; several states in phases 2 and 3 folded others into absent; Nagaland reported absent, shifted and others as a single figure. The table therefore uses the three groups every state reported: dead, shifted, absent or other, and enrolled more than once.

Removed per 100 electors

Names left off the draft roll, divided by the electors on the roll before the revision began. Where that roll was published only in lakh or crore, the rate carries the rounding. Chandigarh's roll before the revision was not published exactly, so it has no rate.

How the table compares with the national totals reported

Where each row comes from

Press note: the Chief Electoral Officer's own document. Press report: the Chief Electoral Officer's figures as reported by a newspaper or agency, linked. Lists: counted on this site.

The final rolls

For each state whose final roll is out: the draft and final rolls, and the names added on claims and removed on objections in between, from one source per state. Adds up: draft plus additions less removals equals the final roll. Net only: the state published the draft and final rolls but not the additions and removals behind the difference, and figures reported as additions were in fact the net rise. Does not add up: the published figures do not reconcile, as the note explains. The fall at the final roll is the names removed at the draft less the rise from draft to final. That rise counts new electors as well as names put back, and no state has said how many of those added had been removed at the draft.

Women among the removed

Ten states have a split by sex: removals by sex where a state published them, or the roll by sex before the revision and on the draft, from which women removed is the difference. Exact: whole numbers from the Chief Electoral Officer or a count of the list. Rounded: published in lakh or crore. Earlier roll: the roll before the revision is an older one than the roll frozen for it. Unverified: from a press analysis that cites no primary source. Bihar is compared with women's share of the 2011 population, because its roll by sex is not in these sources.

The adult population and the 2024 vote

The projected population aged 18 and over on 1 March 2026 is from the Technical Group on Population Projections (National Commission on Population, 2020), which prints it for 18 states; Delhi's is computed from the same report's age tables. The report projects the seven North-Eastern states by age only as a group, and Goa and the smaller union territories by total population only, so those states have no adult figure. The projections start from the 2011 census and count residents. Electors and votes polled in the 2024 Lok Sabha election are from the Election Commission's statistical report of state-wise turnout; Gujarat's exclude Surat, returned unopposed. Rolls before the revision date from June 2025 in Bihar, October 2025 in phase 2 and mid-2026 in phase 3, so the 2026 projection is up to nine months later than some rolls.

State characteristics

Urban share, literacy, SC and ST shares, sex ratio and population are from the 2011 census primary census abstract; the Muslim share from the 2011 religion tables; the two migration measures from the 2011 place-of-birth tables (born in the state and living in another, and living in the state and born in another, each as a share of the state's population). Telangana and Andhra Pradesh are rebuilt from their 2011 districts and have no migration figure, because the 2011 tables count a move between them as a move within one state. Income per head is per capita net state domestic product at current prices for 2023-24, from the Reserve Bank's handbook of state statistics; Lakshadweep and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu have none. International borders are from the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Correlations are rank correlations across states, so a single very large or very small state moves them less than it would a linear correlation. They are still based on a few dozen states at most, and a state-level relationship says nothing about which people within a state were removed.

What the figures cannot show

A reason is what an officer entered after a visit or its absence. It is not a finding that the elector was dead, had moved or was enrolled twice, and the figures cannot say how many entries were wrong. Outside Bihar they are totals published by the states, not counts from the lists, so they cannot be broken down by sex, age, booth or constituency, or checked name by name. Several states' figures were published rounded, some reports of the same state disagree, and three states' reasons leave some names unexplained; each is noted against its row.
