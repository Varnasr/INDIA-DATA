Sources and method

In short

    The Election Commission has published no state-by-state table of the reasons for removal. Each state's Chief Electoral Officer announced its own figures with the draft roll. This page sets out where each row of the All India table comes from and how far each can be trusted.

    What is counted

    Names left off the draft roll

    Each state's figure is the number of electors on the roll before the revision who were not carried onto the draft roll when enumeration ended, with the reason the booth officer gave for each. Every state published a split by reason at that stage. Names removed later, at hearings and on objections before the final roll, are counted separately, because no state has said why they were removed.

    Three groups of reasons

    The enumeration form had five reasons, namely dead, permanently shifted, absent or untraceable, already enrolled elsewhere, and others, which included refusals. States reported them differently. Bihar's phase 1 used three categories, with shifted and absent together. Several states in phases 2 and 3 counted others as absent, and Nagaland gave absent, shifted and others as one figure. So the table uses the three groups every state reported, dead, shifted, absent or other, and enrolled more than once.

    Removed per 100 electors

    Names left off the draft roll, divided by the electors on the roll before the revision began. Where that roll was published only in lakh or crore, the rate is rounded too. Chandigarh did not publish an exact roll from before the revision, so it has no rate.

    How the table compares with the national totals reported

    Where each row comes from

    A press note is the Chief Electoral Officer's own document. A press report gives the Chief Electoral Officer's figures as a newspaper or agency reported them, with a link. Lists means counted on this site.

    The final rolls

    For each state whose final roll is out, this gives the draft and final rolls and the names added on claims and removed on objections in between, from one source per state. Adds up means the draft plus additions less removals equals the final roll. Net only means the state published the draft and final rolls but not the additions and removals behind the difference, and the figure reported as additions was in fact the net rise. Does not add up means the published figures do not match, as the note explains. The fall at the final roll is the names removed at the draft less the rise from draft to final. New electors are part of that rise, and no state has said how many of those added had been removed at the draft.

    Women among the removed

    Ten states have a split by sex: removals by sex where a state published them, or the roll by sex before the revision and on the draft, from which women removed is the difference. Exact: whole numbers from the Chief Electoral Officer or a count of the list. Rounded: published in lakh or crore. Earlier roll: the roll before the revision is an older one than the roll frozen for it. Unverified: from a press analysis that cites no primary source. Bihar's roll before the revision by sex is its draft roll by sex plus the names removed at the draft by sex.

    The adult population and the 2024 vote

    The projected population aged 18 and over on 1 March 2026 is from the Technical Group on Population Projections (National Commission on Population, 2020), which prints it for 18 states; Delhi's is computed from the same report's age tables. The report projects the seven North-Eastern states by age only as a group, and Goa and the smaller union territories by total population only, so those states have no adult figure. The projections start from the 2011 census and count residents. Electors and votes polled in the 2024 Lok Sabha election are from the Election Commission's statistical report of state-wise turnout; Gujarat's exclude Surat, returned unopposed. Rolls before the revision date from June 2025 in Bihar, October 2025 in phase 2 and mid-2026 in phase 3, so the 2026 projection is up to nine months later than some rolls.

    State characteristics

    Literacy for everyone and for women, aged 7 and over, is from the Periodic Labour Force Survey 2023-24 (annual report, appendix table 7). Spending per head, rural and urban, is from the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023-24 (fact sheet, statement 7), the series without the value of free items. The urban share is the Technical Group's projection for 1 March 2026 (table 10). Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu are projected as two territories, so the merged union territory has no figure. The Muslim, SC and ST shares and the sex ratio are from the National Family Health Survey 2019-21. The shares are of households, by the religion and caste of the household head (India report, table 2.10), and run a little below shares of people because Muslim households are larger than average. The sex ratio is of the whole population. The two migration measures are from the 2011 census place-of-birth tables, because the migration survey of 2020-21 was published for India as a whole and not by state. Telangana and Andhra Pradesh have no migration figure, because the 2011 tables count a move between them as a move within one state. Income per head is net state domestic product per head at current prices for 2023-24, from the Reserve Bank's handbook of state statistics. International borders are from the Ministry of Home Affairs.

    Correlations are rank correlations across states, so a single very large or very small state moves them less than it would a linear correlation. They are still based on a few dozen states at most, and a state-level relationship says nothing about which people within a state were removed.

    What the figures cannot show

    A reason is what an officer wrote down after a visit, or after failing to find the elector. Nobody has checked whether the elector was in fact dead, had moved or was enrolled twice, and the figures cannot say how many entries were wrong. Outside Bihar they are totals the states published, so they cannot be split by sex, age, booth or constituency, or checked name by name. Several states published rounded figures, some reports of the same state disagree, and in three states the reasons leave some names unexplained. Each of these is noted against its row. The downloadable table gives every figure with its source.
