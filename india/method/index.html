Sources and method

The Election Commission has published no state-by-state table of the reasons for removal. Each state's Chief Electoral Officer announced its own figures when the draft roll was published. This page says where each row of the All India table comes from, and how far each can be trusted.

What is counted

Names left off the draft roll

Each state's figure is the number of electors on the roll before the revision who were not carried onto the draft roll at the end of enumeration, with the reason the booth officer entered for each. That is the stage at which every state published a split by reason. Names removed later, at hearings and on objections before the final roll, are counted separately, because no state has said why they were removed.

Three groups of reasons

The enumeration form carried five reasons: dead, permanently shifted, absent or untraceable, already enrolled elsewhere, and others (including refusals). States reported them differently. Bihar's phase 1 used three categories, with shifted and absent as one; several states in phases 2 and 3 folded others into absent; Nagaland reported absent, shifted and others as a single figure. The table therefore uses the three groups every state reported: dead, shifted, absent or other, and enrolled more than once.

Removed per 100 electors

Names left off the draft roll, divided by the electors on the roll before the revision began. Where that roll was published only in lakh or crore, the rate carries the rounding. Chandigarh's roll before the revision was not published exactly, so it has no rate.

How the table compares with the national totals reported

Where each row comes from

Press note: the Chief Electoral Officer's own document. Press report: the Chief Electoral Officer's figures as reported by a newspaper or agency, linked. Lists: counted on this site.

What the figures cannot show

A reason is what an officer entered after a visit or its absence. It is not a finding that the elector was dead, had moved or was enrolled twice, and the figures cannot say how many entries were wrong. Outside Bihar they are totals published by the states, not counts from the lists, so they cannot be broken down by sex, age, booth or constituency, or checked name by name. Several states' figures were published rounded, some reports of the same state disagree, and three states' reasons leave some names unexplained; each is noted against its row.
